05 / Flight dynamics & controlEvaluated in simulation

Landing an unpowered flying wing with an LQR elevon controller

With Navid Navidzadeh, turned nonlinear aircraft dynamics into an elevon-feedback landing controller. Tuned control weights and used randomized approaches and failure analysis to assess the limits of the local linear model.

87.3% landing success across 4,000 simulated trials

LQRAircraft trimState-space modeling
06 / Spacecraft controlEvaluated in simulation

Stabilizing a spacecraft hatch with reaction wheels and a star-based observer

With Adrian Huang, combined reaction-wheel feedback and noisy star measurements to align a spacecraft hatch for astronaut entry. Separate estimation and mission tests revealed the limits of the integrated design.

64% mission success; illustrated observer trials below the 3.5° error limit

LQRState estimationReaction wheels
07 / Robot feedback controlSimulated controller

Balancing a wheeled robot through a cat-catching impact with state feedback

With Luke Phan, designed wheel-torque feedback to balance a robot before and after catching a falling cat in simulation. Checked candidate gains for local stability, implemented torque limits, and examined the residual offsets after impact.

One documented successful catch; post-impact equilibrium offsets identified

State feedbackEigenvalue analysisTorque limits
08 / Computational heat transferCompleted team study

Verifying a layered heat-shield model for reentry conduction

Checked and explained the numerical evidence behind a team model of reentry heating through a layered heat shield. Connected the implementation to manufactured-solution convergence and an analytical benchmark, then interpreted the tradeoff between surface heating and heat penetration.

Near-second-order convergence; 0.0161 K maximum benchmark discrepancy

Finite volumesCrank–NicolsonManufactured solutions

Modeling balloon ascent to compare hydrogen and helium for a 35 km mission

Built a Python model connecting atmospheric conditions, buoyancy, balloon expansion, and burst limits to a gas-cost decision for a 3 kg payload and 35 km target.

12 / Composite materials testingCompleted laboratory study

Comparing composite tensile behavior with extensometer and DIC strain

Compared contact and optical strain measurements in graphite/epoxy tensile tests at 0°, ±45°, and 90°. My analysis connected orientation-dependent performance with measurement uncertainty and the limits of ideal laminate predictions.

0° coupon reached 807.7 MPa; directional trends were clear, but ideal predictions did not match quantitatively

13 / Experimental modal analysisCompleted laboratory study

Identifying cantilever modes with shaker and impact-hammer tests

Connected visible beam motion to frequency-domain measurements, then reconstructed five mode shapes from 17 impact locations. Manual resonance selections agreed with EDM Modal results within 0.4% for every measured mode.

Identified five impact-test modes; manual and EDM frequencies differed by less than 0.4%

Modal analysisImpact hammerFrequency response
14 / Experimental stress analysisCompleted laboratory study

Measuring notch stresses with photoelasticity and FEA

Used visible fringe patterns to investigate how U- and V-notches concentrate bending stress. Comparing measurements, analytical estimates, and Abaqus output exposed a geometry limit in the V-notch approximation.

Visualized notch stress concentrations and identified a limit in the V-notch approximation

PhotoelasticityFour-point bendingStress concentration
15 / Structural stability testingCompleted laboratory study

Testing how supports, material, and eccentricity affect rod buckling

Compared compression tests with Euler buckling predictions while changing supports, material, and load eccentricity. The fixed–fixed test fell well below its ideal prediction, making restraint quality a central part of the analysis.

Measured stiffness trends supported Euler theory; the fixed–fixed test exposed restraint sensitivity

Euler bucklingForce–deflectionBoundary conditions
